# 1.vision.experience.dimensions — the walked product

per `howto.experience.decompose` + `rule.require.dimensional-decomposition`. the experience space is
walked as **two orthogonal sub-products**, split on the one latent dimension that genuinely
partitions the actors. every cell in each sub-product carries a verdict.

## the decomposition, in two corrections (r1 + r2 peer review)

**r1 correction — five axes → two.** an earlier draft declared five axes (actor · moment ·
defect-class · repo-state · tier), a 1200-cell product it refused to walk. three of those were
*derived*, not free: **tier** is implied by the defect (a stage-divergence fault shows only where
`access≠stage` = prep), **defect-class** is the roster-root label, **repo-state** is implied by the
moment. they are demoted to per-cell **derived attributes** below.

**r2 correction — surface the latent actor dimension.** a single actor × moment grid still hid a
correlation: a consumer/operator never authors a practice declaration, and a maintainer who writes one
makes no runtime cross-service call. that correlation is a **latent dimension** — *which side of the
practice boundary the actor stands on*: an **author** who writes declarations, or a **consumer** who
receives and runs them. the two sides have **disjoint moment-sets**, so to force them into one grid
produced impossible-by-correlation cells. the fix is to make that latent dimension the **partition**:
two orthogonal sub-products, each with a shared moment-set every actor in it can meet — so neither
sub-product has an impossible-by-correlation cell.

## sub-product A — the practice **consumers** (runtime side)

actors who *receive and run* the templates. both actors can meet every moment (an operator upgrades,
calls peers, migrates, and reads plan; a consumer-dev likewise), so the grid is genuinely orthogonal —
no actor value bars a moment value.

| axis | values (closed set) |
|---|---|
| **actor** | consumer-dev · operator |
| **moment** | upgrade (`declapract apply`) · cross-service-call · migrate-a-service · run-a-check |

## sub-product B — the practice **authors** (declaration-write side)

the actor who *writes the declarations themselves*. this side's moments are disjoint from the
consumer side — an author does not upgrade or make a runtime call as part of the write. the second
axis that separates the maintainer's encounters cleanly is **which guard the author writes**.

| axis | values (closed set) |
|---|---|
| **actor** | practice-maintainer |
| **authored-guard** | value-rewrite-guard · fix-output-guard · silent-check-guard |

**1 × 3 = 3 cells, walked in full:**

| actor ↓ / authored-guard → | value-rewrite-guard | fix-output-guard | silent-check-guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **practice-maintainer** | **demoed** case=4 · sharp·crit (D27); also hosts itemized **row 17** (#544 config-param derive — a value the template cannot verify, same guard as case=4) | **demoed** case=5 · sharp·crit (D51); also hosts itemized **row 16** (#519/#523/#539/#562 vpc-tunnel — a shallow-merge fix that clobbers the repo's shape, same guard as case=5) | **demoed** case=6 · sharp·crit — the author-audit POV (`[t4]`, "a maintainer audits the plan for blind checks") |

3 cells, all occupiable, none impossible. demoed: case=4, case=5, and case=6 (its author-audit POV).
the value-rewrite-guard cell also hosts itemized row 17; the fix-output-guard cell also hosts row 16 —
the two absorbed clusters that fold into an extant author-side fail-safe rather than owe a new demo.

> **case=6 spans both sub-products by design.** the "silent check speaks" behavior has two POVs of the
> same `run-a-check` event: the **consumer** sees the check refuse to pass silently (sub-product A,
> CD × run-a-check), and the **maintainer** audits the plan for blind checks (sub-product B, PM ×
> silent-check-guard, the `[t4]` step). one demo file carries both, which is why case=6's header lists
> both actors — the walk and the demo agree: case=6 is demoed in one cell of each sub-product.

## why the split is the clean fix (not a dodge)

the partition IS the surfaced latent dimension the rule asks for. within each sub-product the two
axes are independent (every actor meets every moment / every guard), so there is no
impossible-by-correlation cell in either. the author/consumer boundary is not smuggled into the actor
axis — it is the explicit top-level split. the two sub-products together cover all 8 demoed critipaths
(A: 1,2,3,6,7,8; B: 4,5,6 — case=6 and case=8 each span cells, one POV per side) plus the itemized
folds, with no cell left un-verdicted.

## the forbidden states — rejection invariants (these MUST NOT occur)

not actor×moment cells; they are **states the design forbids** (the `forbidden` verdict names an
invariant, per the howto). listed so the rejection contract is explicit:

| the forbidden state | why forbidden |
|---|---|
| a stateful store (RDS/DynamoDB/SQS) published as **two copies** under two names | write-dev/read-prep split-brain — must be one store, two pointers (case=7) |
| a value-rewrite that **guesses** an axis (post-collapse) | after the collapse the enum has one answer (`'prep'`); a `-dev-` target is authored via the typed `sdkAwsLambdaEnvAccessAncient` helper, never a bare rewritten string (case=4) |
| a `fix` whose output lands the file in a state another practice **forbids** | D51 — the one-way door; a shallow-merge that clobbers a repo's shape (#539) is an instance (case=5) |
| a config param that goes **bare** before its value lives at the derived path | `getConfig()` throws on the absent param — move first, rewrite second (#544, row 17) |
| `serverless.yml` `provider.runtime` major **≠** `.nvmrc` major | build-node/deploy-node skew — a lambda that cannot parse (#552, case=8) |
| a new `accessByStage` remap after the axis is collapsed | re-splits the axis the guard exists to hold |
| `--stage` that carries a value **divergent** from `access` | the whole disease; `--stage ${access}` only |

## the boundary-density rank

`define.experience._.metric=boundary-density` — center the cell that touches the most contract
boundaries. ranked:

1. **case 1 (A · CD × upgrade)** — touches env vocab, serverless names, IAM, cross-service. the
   densest; the whole disease meets the whole cure in one walk.
2. **case 3 (A · OP × migrate-a-service)** — deploy + suite + ledger; the operator's arc, and the
   cell that also hosts the sharp case=7.
3. **case 2 (A · CD × cross-service-call)** — the transition's core promise; two services, two slugs,
   one ledger.
4. **case 4/5/6/7/8 (the sharp fail-safes)** — each a boundary between a silent value-emit the practice
   cannot verify ("guesses / hides / copies / skews") and a guard that makes it loud or unreachable
   ("designs out / speaks / aliases / runs-on-the-deploy-node"). case=4's boundary is now the axis
   collapse itself (the enum has one answer), not a marker that refuses to guess.
