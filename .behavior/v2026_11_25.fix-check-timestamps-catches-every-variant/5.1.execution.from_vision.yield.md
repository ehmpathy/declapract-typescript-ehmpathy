## what landed

- `src/practices/husky/best-practice/.husky/check.timestamps.sh` — the pattern is now ten named
  forms (`FORM_T_COLON` … `FORM_END_OF_DAY`), one comment each, joined into `TIMESTAMP_PATTERN`.
  posix ere, no backrefs.
  - a date anchor reads year-, month-, or day-first dates, numeric or with a month name, and a clock
    after it may follow up to three blanks, underscores, or dashes, or `, `, ` at `, ` @ `, ` (`;
    a time range after a date halts too.
  - a `T` before a zoned basic clock, and a lowercase `t` after a date, anchor a clock.
  - `FORM_END_OF_DAY` halts an anchored `24:00` (row 22b); a bare one passes.
  - the duration strip has a left edge, so a word that ends in `P` (`SEPThh:mm`) is no longer eaten.
  - a sed or grep fault halts with `💥 MalfunctionError` (exit 1), so a broken pattern cannot
    disable the guard.
  - the scan runs in the C locale, so a file with one byte that is not utf-8 is still scanned
    (it was skipped whole as binary).
  - a one-digit hour after `[` passes (a slice), and a dotted number then `.digit` after a date
    passes (a version).
  - the halt message and `.ts`/`.sh` skip are unchanged; the header's `.what` names any join,
    anchor, and zone.
- `src/practices/husky/check.timestamps.play.declapract.integration.test.ts`:

  - `[case18]` clamps the grep- and sed-fault halts.
  - `[case19]` walks a clock on line 5 of an older file: halt names line 5, the mask passes, and a

  - `[case14]` also gains a slice and a version-after-date pass row.
